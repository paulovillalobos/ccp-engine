# Real procurement questions outgrow AMALIA's templates

Real people asking about the Código dos Contratos Públicos (CCP) in Portuguese do not write fact sheets: the best real corpus available (CCDR legal-opinion requests from câmaras and juntas) shows questions built around a legal doubt, where the object of the contract is described in everyday words, the value appears as "valor", "ronda os" or a range rather than "preço base", the entity is implied by who is asking, and JOUE publication, territory and the procedure's start date are almost never stated. Typed Portuguese adds a measured noise layer dominated by dropped accents (about 38-51% of all spelling errors, 1.8-4.8 errors per 100 words in Brazilian typed corpora), and dictation adds 12.5-19.2% word error in European Portuguese. On top of that, users bring dated thresholds (the 1 October 2026 reform made the 2018-2026 figures wrong overnight), loose procedure names (bare "concurso", "requisição") and value terms that the law now separates (valor estimado vs optional preço base). The current generator, `tools/make_user_facts.py`, covers none of this: it produces clean, fully accented, fact-dense sentences from one skeleton family, and its held-out split swaps vocabulary inside that same skeleton, which is why a score near 100% on template wordings coexists with 15% exact match (6/40, 95% CI 7.1-29.1%) on hand-written questions. The fix that the evidence supports is a compositional generator that first samples a fact plan (each field stated, implied, ambiguous, contradictory, premise-only or absent, at realistic rates), then realises it through many independent shapes, wrappers, value and date formats, distractors and noise styles, with an extended output schema that marks ambiguity and contradiction instead of guessing. The hand-written test set must also change: it is clean, short, over-rich in facts, contains no contradiction, no IVA, no typo, and is too small to separate a 15% model from a 30% one.

## Officials write long, implicit questions that leave four of seven fields unstated

**Sourced.** The richest open corpus of real written CCP questions is the CCDR legal-opinion archive. CCDR Alentejo often transcribes the câmara's or junta's question verbatim after a stock formula ("Solicitou a Câmara Municipal de ... parecer jurídico ... sobre a seguinte questão") and runs from 2005 to late 2025 ([CCDR-A parecer 1300](https://www.ccdr-a.gov.pt/app_ext/pareceres/printpdf.php?id=1300)). About 35 of roughly 800 retrievable documents in the scanned range concern procurement. The askers are câmaras, juntas and uniões de freguesia, comunidades intermunicipais and bodies such as a Região de Turismo, writing by ofício or email ([parecer 737](https://www.ccdr-a.gov.pt/app_ext/pareceres/printpdf.php?id=737); [parecer 705](https://www.ccdr-a.gov.pt/app_ext/pareceres/printpdf.php?id=705); [parecer 1200](https://www.ccdr-a.gov.pt/app_ext/pareceres/printpdf.php?id=1200)). CCDR Lisboa e Vale do Tejo uses a "QUESTÃO" block with numbered, nested sub-questions ([CCDR-LVT Parecer 72/2011](https://www.ccdr-lvt.pt/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/532d4f2ce29c1dedd33f5248fc3984496322de9e.pdf)). Suppliers' questions appear in published pedidos de esclarecimento, in an informal-commercial register that mixes complaint and demand ("O Valor base é claramente insuficiente. Aguardamos retificação") ([Município de Lamego](https://www.cm-lamego.pt/cmlamego/uploads/document/file/2385/esclarecimentos.pdf)). Official FAQs such as Portal BASE's 54 headings are editorial and definitional ("Quando é que se pode adotar o procedimento de ajuste direto?"), with every procedure fact missing ([BASE perguntas frequentes](https://www.base.gov.pt/Base4/pt/perguntas-frequentes/)); the Segurança Social FAQ reuses that wording and adds the beneficiary's doubt "Como saber se somos enquadrados como entidade adjudicante ou não adjudicante?" ([Seg. Social FAQ](https://sisscontent.seg-social.pt/documents/10152/18433474/FAQ%27+s+_+Contra%C3%A7%C3%A3o+P%C3%BAblica_Mobilidade+Verde+Social.pdf/49e26ced-3dae-4d17-b011-e5f3ca4d6251)).

The register of official questions is formal-administrative, sometimes citing a CPV code or an EU regulation, and length runs from one sentence to multi-paragraph ofícios with numbered points ([parecer 1047](https://www.ccdr-a.gov.pt/app_ext/pareceres/printpdf.php?id=1047); [parecer 1023](https://www.ccdr-a.gov.pt/app_ext/pareceres/printpdf.php?id=1023)). They are often vague enough that CCDR says so: Marvão "não emitiu o seu entendimento", so the opinion "será necessariamente genérico" ([parecer 737](https://www.ccdr-a.gov.pt/app_ext/pareceres/printpdf.php?id=737)). Five shapes recur: **(A) context then an open "which procedure?"** (Marvão lists purchases, then asks "que procedimentos são necessários?"); **(B) a yes/no "can we?" with an embedded value** (a €19,000 van; an ajuste direto for a contract between €25k and €75k); **(C) numbered multi-question ofícios with conditional branches** ("Caso a resposta seja não..."); **(D) "is our interpretation right?"**, with the asker's own reading or an attached ANMP/ANAFRE note; and **(E) a supplier complaint about the preço base** ([737](https://www.ccdr-a.gov.pt/app_ext/pareceres/printpdf.php?id=737); [804](https://www.ccdr-a.gov.pt/app_ext/pareceres/printpdf.php?id=804); [565](https://www.ccdr-a.gov.pt/app_ext/pareceres/printpdf.php?id=565); [CCDR-LVT 72/2011](https://www.ccdr-lvt.pt/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/532d4f2ce29c1dedd33f5248fc3984496322de9e.pdf); [985](https://www.ccdr-a.gov.pt/app_ext/pareceres/printpdf.php?id=985); [Lamego](https://www.cm-lamego.pt/cmlamego/uploads/document/file/2385/esclarecimentos.pdf)).

Field by field, the real questions behave very differently from the generator's sentences. **preco_base** is rarely called "preço base" by officials: values come as "cada aquisição não chega a 4.000 €", "ronda os € 70 000", "entre € 25 000 e € 75 000", per item rather than per contract, or with malformed separators such as "até 149,639,37" ([737](https://www.ccdr-a.gov.pt/app_ext/pareceres/printpdf.php?id=737); [728](https://www.ccdr-a.gov.pt/app_ext/pareceres/printpdf.php?id=728); [565](https://www.ccdr-a.gov.pt/app_ext/pareceres/printpdf.php?id=565); [1323](https://www.ccdr-a.gov.pt/app_ext/pareceres/printpdf.php?id=1323)). **tipo_contrato** is usually implied by the object (viatura usada, brita, carrinha, limpeza) and is sometimes the very thing in doubt: the excavator-with-operator case asks whether it is aquisição de serviços or empreitada, and the labour case asks about "fornecimento em contínuo de serviços de mão-de-obra" for paving ([1047](https://www.ccdr-a.gov.pt/app_ext/pareceres/printpdf.php?id=1047); [1023](https://www.ccdr-a.gov.pt/app_ext/pareceres/printpdf.php?id=1023)). **tipo_procedimento** is stated when the doubt is about a named procedure and absent when the asker wants it chosen ("qual o procedimento a adoptar") ([801](https://www.ccdr-a.gov.pt/app_ext/pareceres/printpdf.php?id=801); [1047](https://www.ccdr-a.gov.pt/app_ext/pareceres/printpdf.php?id=1047)). **tipo_entidade** is always implicit in the asker's identity, and no inspected asker ever wrote "Estado" ([705](https://www.ccdr-a.gov.pt/app_ext/pareceres/printpdf.php?id=705); [1200](https://www.ccdr-a.gov.pt/app_ext/pareceres/printpdf.php?id=1200)). **publicado_joue, territorio and data_inicio_procedimento were stated in none of the inspected questions**; time is anchored through law references and years ("novo Código", "no ano de 2008 ... no ano de 2009", "Decreto-Lei n.º 108/2024"), and territory only through which CCDR answers ([616](https://www.ccdr-a.gov.pt/app_ext/pareceres/printpdf.php?id=616); [1315](https://www.ccdr-a.gov.pt/app_ext/pareceres/printpdf.php?id=1315)). Older texts use pre-Acordo spelling ("directo", "adoptar") and colloquial names ("ajuste directo simplificado (vulgo requisição)") ([CCDR-LVT 72/2011](https://www.ccdr-lvt.pt/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/532d4f2ce29c1dedd33f5248fc3984496322de9e.pdf)).

**Inferred.** For a generator, the official corpus says: drop most fields by default, express the entity through self-identification, express the contract type through an object phrase, express value through several terms and loose forms, and anchor time through years and law names that must not become a start date. Shapes A and C are the most common in official writing and C forces the parser to cope with several procedures in one message.

**Unknown.** No informal citizen or SME corpus was inspected: Reddit refused automated access, and Portuguese forums, LinkedIn groups, Ciberdúvidas and the OCC consultório yielded nothing usable. CCDR Centro, Norte and Algarve pareceres were not retrievable. The real-question evidence is therefore biased toward local-government officials, shape frequencies come from about 30 documents rather than a count, and no word-length statistics exist. No reuse licence was found on any of these sources, so they suit short-phrase lexicon mining and internal evaluation, not training text.

## Accents vanish first: the measured shape of Portuguese typing and dictation noise

**Sourced.** The best quantified typed-error data is Brazilian Portuguese. Gimenes, Roman and Carvalho counted non-word errors in 62,858 browser-typed words without a spell-checker (C1) and 26,418 blog words (C2): **1.81 and 4.77 errors per 100 words**. Missing diacritics were **37.66% and 45.48%** of errors, omissions 17.12% and 24.13%, substitutions 12.82% and 5.79%, insertions 10.45% and 9.44%, missing cedilla 6.06% and 4.44%, transpositions 3.69% and 0.24%, and glued words 2.37% in C1. **92.3% and 86.3% of misspelled words carried exactly one error.** The distribution did not differ significantly from Spanish, and the authors tie it to the character set rather than the language, which is why it transfers to European Portuguese's identical accent inventory ([Gimenes et al. 2015](https://aclanthology.org/J15-1011.pdf)). European Portuguese studies point the same way without frequencies: in Medeiros (1995), 80.1% of errors fit Damerau's four edit types, and higher-education writers most often omit accents on proparoxytones ([J15-1011](https://aclanthology.org/J15-1011.pdf); [Castelo & Sousa, UAb](https://repositorioaberto.uab.pt/bitstream/10400.2/11999/1/Castelo__Sousa_Desempenhoortogrficoemestudantesdoensinosuperior-aacentuaogrfica.pdf)).

User-generated reviews add the hardest class. **About 25,000 Portuguese word pairs differ only by a diacritic**, so a dropped accent often yields another real word, and a normaliser recovered only **34% of such real-word errors** against 84% of non-word errors. Hand-annotated review samples show misspellings at 3.0-4.7% of tokens, punctuation errors at 1.5-2.0%, case errors at 0.8% and slang at 0.2-0.6% ([Duran et al. 2015](https://aclanthology.org/W15-4305.pdf); [Duran et al. 2014](https://aclanthology.org/W14-0404/)). Frequent Portuguese confusions include "à muito tempo" for "há muito tempo" ([e-konomista](https://www.e-konomista.pt/erros-ortograficos/)).

Spelling reform splits the population. The 1990 Acordo became mandatory in Portugal on 13 May 2015 and changed about 1.5-1.6% of words, mostly silent consonants (acção→ação, directo→direto, objecto→objeto, projecto→projeto); a petition against it reached nearly 100,000 signatures, and journals report manuscripts arriving in mixed spellings ([IJNet](https://ijnet.org/en/node/2691); [Observador](https://observador.pt/2016/01/01/acordo-ortografico-falantes-portugues-sobem-215-milhoes/); [PJP](https://ojs.pjp.spp.pt/article/download/3075/2480/7252)). Brazilian procurement vocabulary belongs to a different legal system: Lei 14.133/2021 speaks of licitação, pregão, concorrência, dispensa and inexigibilidade, none of which maps one-to-one onto CCP procedures ([ConLicitação](https://conlicitacao.com.br/quais-sao-as-modalidades-de-licitacao/)).

For dictation, the CAMÕES benchmark measured **19.2% average WER for zero-shot Whisper large-v3 on European Portuguese and 12.5% after fine-tuning on 425 hours**, with hallucinations on non-speech segments and poor transfer across Portuguese varieties; because its normaliser strips casing and punctuation, real dictated text is noisier than these figures ([CAMÕES, arXiv 2508.19721](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19721v1)).

**Inferred.** The generator should treat "casual" typing as about 4.8 errors per 100 words and "careful" as about 1.8, with accent stripping as the dominant operator, and real-word accent swaps (é/e, à/há/a, público/publico, está/esta, têm/tem) as their own class because they are the hardest to normalise. Pre-AO spelling is a whole-question style, not per-word noise. Procurement acronyms (AD, CP, CLPQ, CCP, JOUE/JO, PB, CE, s/IVA, c/IVA, art.º, n.º), money forms ("5k", "75 mil", "1,5 M€", "1.500,00 €", "m€") and date forms ("15/01/27", "dia 15", "daqui a 10 dias", "próxima segunda") are inventories compiled from domain knowledge; their frequencies are not measured. Dictation should be simulated as 10-20% word corruption plus lowercase, missing punctuation, spelled-out numbers and split acronyms ("cê pê", "a dê").

**Unknown.** No European Portuguese typed-error frequency table, keyboard-adjacency matrix, dead-key error rate, pre-AO usage share in current informal text, procurement-shorthand frequency, number-format error rate, or ASR error-type breakdown for adult dictated questions was found. The PT-PT keyboard detail (ç to the right of L, dead accent keys near Enter) is general knowledge that was not verified.

## Users bring dated thresholds and loose terms the extractor must not adjudicate

**Sourced (law, from the project's dated DR capture and law-firm notes).** DL 177/2026, published 4 September 2026 and in force on 1 October 2026, applies to procedures "iniciados após" that date, raised the national thresholds and changed the value concept ([CMS](https://cms.law/pt/prt/news-information/alteracao-ao-codigo-dos-contratos-publicos); [ECO](https://eco.sapo.pt/descodificador/12-perguntas-e-respostas-para-perceber-as-novas-regras-da-contratacao-publica/); DR capture `law/passagem/dl-177-2026-art10.r1.json`). Ajuste direto moved from below €30,000 to below **€150,000 for obras** and from €20,000 to **€75,000 for bens and serviços**; consulta prévia from €150,000 to **€1,000,000** and from €75,000 to **€130,000** (DR capture `ccp-art19.c.r2.json`, `ccp-art19.d.r2.json`, `ccp-art20.1.c.r2.json`, `ccp-art20.1.d.r2.json`; [CMS](https://cms.law/pt/prt/news-information/alteracao-ao-codigo-dos-contratos-publicos)). Users can therefore carry at least three dated regimes in their heads: pre-2018 (no consulta prévia in arts. 19.º/20.º), 2018 to 30 September 2026, and from 1 October 2026. Headlines seed new errors: "ajustes diretos podem chegar aos 150 mil euros" is true only for obras ([Postal](https://postal.pt/nacional/nova-lei-da-contratacao-publica-entra-em-vigor-ajustes-diretos-podem-chegar-aos-150-mil-euros/)). The reform replaced "valor do contrato" with "valor estimado do contrato", which now "condiciona a escolha do procedimento", while preço base became something the entity "pode fixar" (DR capture `ccp-art17.r2.json`, `ccp-art47.1.r2.json`). All CCP amounts exclude IVA (art. 473.º, DR capture `ccp-art473.r2.json`). The procedure "inicia-se com a decisão de contratar" (art. 36.º, n.º 1, DR capture `ccp-art36.1.r2.json`), not with the anúncio or the contract. Art. 22.º on lots was revoked and replaced by art. 17.º-B on fracionamento (DR capture `ccp-art22.1.r1.json`, `ccp-art17-b.r2.json`). Concurso público exists both with and without a JOUE announcement (DR capture `ccp-art19.r2.json`, `ccp-art20.r2.json`), and art. 474.º, n.º 3 separates contracts "adjudicados pelo Estado" from other entities (DR capture `ccp-art474.r2.json`). Practice confirms misuse: auditors found irregularities in 85% of 81 ajuste-direto files at Águas de Portugal, and artificial splitting led to 100% financial corrections ([ECO 2017](https://eco.sapo.pt/2017/05/10/aguas-de-portugal-85-dos-ajustes-diretos-tem-irregularidades/); [CIG 2021](https://www.cig.gov.pt/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Informacao-de-gestao_8-de-junho-2021.pdf)). Real askers also argue opposite readings of the same rule: Elvas held that art. 113.º/2 limits do not apply to the simplified ajuste direto, while a 2023 câmara called it "claro" that they do ([801](https://www.ccdr-a.gov.pt/app_ext/pareceres/printpdf.php?id=801); [1262](https://www.ccdr-a.gov.pt/app_ext/pareceres/printpdf.php?id=1262)).

**Sourced (QA research).** In CREPE, **about a quarter of 8,400 real Reddit questions contain false presuppositions**, and models detect them moderately but struggle to verify them because verification needs evidence ([CREPE](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.583)). FalseQA reports that **about 256 fine-tuning examples** teach false-premise discrimination, and mixing ordinary questions in prevents over-rejection ([Hu et al. 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.309)). CLAMBER and ClarifyMT-Bench find models under-identify ambiguity and answer prematurely ([CLAMBER](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.12063v2); [ClarifyMT-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.21120v1)).

**Inferred.** The extractor must keep two kinds of thing apart. Facts about this procedure ("vamos fazer consulta prévia para uma carrinha") are extracted as stated, even when the user's legal conclusion is wrong. Claims about the law ("o limite do AD é 20 mil", "não precisa de ir ao JOUE, diz o colega", "por ser urgente") are never facts. Because the same sentence ("ajuste direto até 20 mil") was right on 30 September 2026 and wrong for bens on 1 October, judging a premise needs the dated law, which is the engine's job; the engine can correct an outdated threshold simply by stating the right one, so the extractor does not need to output the premise at all. The line the extractor may cross is lexical or geographic **mapping** ("Ponta Delgada"→PT-AC, "ministério"→estado, "empreitada"→obras); the line it may not cross is **legal classification** (whether a universidade is "Estado", whether refeições escolares are bens or serviços, whether an amount with IVA can stand for preço base, whether a launch date is the decisão de contratar). Where a term maps to several enum values, or two values clash, the right output is a marker, not a guess, because the evidence says models under-flag and a guessed value becomes a wrong legal answer downstream.

**Unknown.** No public-forum user question showing these errors verbatim was found; the conflation of cabimento or orçamento with preço base, and IVA-inclusive amounts, rest on the law text plus inference. The EU threshold for Estado bens/serviços conflicts between the republished art. 474.º (€139,000) and a law-firm reading of Regulation 2025/2152 (€140,000) ([Cuatrecasas](https://www.cuatrecasas.com/pt/portugal/publico-1/art/novos-limiares-ue-contratacao-publica-2026-2027-1); DR capture `ccp-art474.3.b.r2.json`); this is an engine question. How a procedure whose decisão de contratar falls on 1 October 2026 itself is classed was not found in any commentary.

## Template diversity, not volume, closes the 100%-to-15% gap

**Sourced (literature).** Character noise transfers to real noise only under some recipes. Belinkov and Bisk found that training on swap, scramble or keyboard noise alone left natural-error BLEU near the clean baseline (16.4-16.9 vs 17.42), while mixed training including natural errors reached 31.77; natural errors are mostly phonological changes and omissions that scramblers never produce ([Belinkov & Bisk 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1711.02173)). NAT, closer to slot filling, used uniform insert/delete/substitute noise at 10% with one noisy copy per clean sentence, found the best range at **10-30% noise**, and lifted real-OCR NER from 76.44 to 84.79 with clean accuracy intact ([NAT](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2005.07162)). In Noise-SF, a clean-trained slot filler lost 31.5 F1 on typos, 13.6 on verbosity, 9.9 on simplification and 7.7 on paraphrase, and rule-based word operations did not fix sentence-level noise ([Noise-SF](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.03518)). Herzig and Berant showed that generate-then-paraphrase data mismatches real usage in both what is asked and how it is phrased (51.3% vs 70.6%) ([Herzig & Berant 2019](https://aclanthology.org/D19-1394/)); grammar-generated data came within 5-6 points of human data only with rich compositional templates ([AutoQA](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.31)). LAUG's augmentation lifted perturbed-set F1 from 86.95 to 91.06, but real users stayed far harder (65.22 to 69.12) ([LAUG](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.15262v3)). Diverse data improves worst-case instruction following ([Bukharin et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.14736)), and LoRA learns less from target data than full fine-tuning at low rank but forgets less ([Biderman et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09673v2)). Simulated noise overstates robustness relative to real noise ([NoiseBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07609v2)).

**Sourced (project files, measured here).** The current generator draws one skeleton family: an optional entity clause joined by "e" to a "vamos lançar X para Y em Z" core, then shuffled stock sentences for price, JOUE, date and an aside, then a stock final question; held-out swaps in disjoint vocabulary and two layouts but keeps the same frames, the same clean register and the same 13 fixed prices (`tools/make_user_facts.py`). D50 itself diagnoses the earlier 400/400 as "one sentence pattern" (`schemas/DECISIONS.md`). Compared with the 40 hand-written questions, generated questions are longer (mean 30.4 words train, 36.1 held-out, vs 20.9) and denser: entity is stated in 60% vs 32.5%, start date in 45% vs 32.5%, and questions with at most one fact are 3.2% of train vs 25% of the hand-written set. Generated text has no typo, no stripped accent, no lowercase start, no IVA, no "valor", no range, no relative date other than "hoje"/"amanhã", no contradiction and no ambiguity marker. On disk, the measurement that matches the 10%/15% figures is `training/runs/v5/BEFORE.md`: base AMALIA 4/40 exact, base plus v4 adapter 6/40; no post-training measurement of the v5 adapter was found. Per-field breakdown of those readings (`governance/readings/avaliacao-v5/factos_reais/leitor-a5.jsonl`) shows the v4-adapted reader **missed tipo_contrato in 27 of 30 questions that implied it through an object**, missed JOUE in 10 of 13 and entity in 11 of 13, and over-extracted by default-filling: **today's date as start date 8 times, preco_base 0 four times, and a guessed procedure on five questions that named none**; it also mapped Bragança and Coimbra to PT-AC. Overall, 26.5% of its predicted facts were unsupported and 50.8% of stated facts were missing.

**Inferred.** Typos are not why the hand-written score is 15%: the hand-written set contains essentially no noise (one "??"). The gap is structural, matching Herzig and Berant's two mismatches: the model has not learned to map objects to contract types, places to territories and self-descriptions to entities, and it has learned (or kept from base) the habit of filling every field. A grammar that separates the fact plan from its surface realisation, with many frames, verbosity wrappers, telegraphic forms, distractor numbers and dates, and explicit negative cases, attacks exactly those failures; noise operators are a second layer that helps on real typed and dictated input. Capping instances per skeleton is the LoRA-appropriate way to spend capacity.

**Unknown.** No study measures synthetic-to-natural typo transfer for a 7-9B decoder under LoRA, how many examples each noise class needs, or whether an ambiguity marker beats plain omission for LLM JSON extraction. All rates below are proposals to be tuned on a dev set.

## Recommendations

Owners follow the project convention ("Build:" does code work; "Paulo:" decides product rules and supplies human-written text). Every rate in this section is **inferred** unless a source is named in the anchor column.

### 1. Output schema: add markers, keep facts strict

Keep `factos` exactly as now. Add two optional objects: `ambiguos` (field → list of candidate values, or the stated raw amount/date for value fields) and `contraditorios` (field → the two stated values). A field that appears in either object never appears in `factos`. Premises about the law produce no output; the generator records them only in example metadata (`fenomenos`) for evaluation. A question where the user says they do not know ("ainda não sabemos se vai ao JOUE", "concurso ou ajuste direto?") is **not stated**, not ambiguous: the user has told us the fact is open. Paulo: approve the schema change, because the engine must read the markers (it can then ask a question, or answer conditionally when all candidates give the same result).

### 2. Generator categories and rates

The generator should run in four layers, each sampled independently: fact plan, question shape, wrapper/register, surface noise. Field-status rates per question:

| Field | Stated (plain or mapped) | Ambiguous | Contradictory | Absent (incl. user says unknown) | Anchor |

| tipo_entidade | 35% (câmara, junta, ministério, direção-geral, "somos um município") | 8% (instituto, universidade, escola, hospital, empresa municipal, Governo Regional, "somos do Estado") | 1% | 56% | CCDR: entity implicit in asker; never "Estado" |
| tipo_procedimento | 40% named | 10% (bare "concurso", "requisição", "concurso internacional", "AD com três propostas") | 2% | 48% (15% explicitly "que procedimento?") | CCDR shapes A/B |

### 5. Corrections to the train/held-out split

Split by skeleton, not by instance or vocabulary item alone: give each frame, wrapper, shape and ordering an ID, and hold out whole families (for example all of shape D, all email wrappers, two of the eight entity frames). Hold out unseen field combinations (for example JOUE with ajuste direto, territory with no entity). Hold out noise operators never used in training (OCR-style rn/m and l/1 confusions, an unseen abbreviation list, an unseen ASR homophone list). Replace the 13 fixed prices with random values so held-out values are new numbers. Report in-grammar held-out only as a regression check; it must never be the decision metric. Keep 5-10% replay of earlier tasks to limit behavioural narrowing, and compare LoRA rank 16 vs 64 on the dev set, since added surface diversity may exceed low-rank capacity ([Biderman et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09673v2)).

### 6. Evaluation design

| Set | Purpose | Size | Labels |
|---|---|---|---|
| In-grammar held-out (skeleton- and combination-split) | regression | 400 | code |
| Held-out noise operators | transfer to unseen noise | 400 | code |
| Invariance and directional tests on the human set | behaviour checks | about 5 variants per item | code: accent strip, lowercase, reorder, typo, distractor date must not change the JSON; adding "não", a second value or "com IVA" must change it as expected |
| Human dev set | error analysis, lexicon mining | 100 | human |
| Human final set + factos-v1 | decision metric | 100 + 40 | human |

Metrics, all with Wilson intervals and broken down by phenomenon tag: per-field precision, recall and F1 with the field-value pair as unit; exact JSON match as secondary; **over-extraction rate** (predicted facts not supported, currently 26.5% for the v4-adapted reader) reported separately because an invented fact is worse than a missing one for a law engine; omission rate (currently 50.8%); wrong-value rate; value-normalisation accuracy for amounts and dates; marker precision and recall, plus the false-flag rate on questions with no ambiguity; and a default-fill counter (start date equal to "hoje" when not stated, preco_base 0, procedure on a fact-free question). Report a training-mixture by test-noise matrix, adding or removing one noise class at a time ([Belinkov & Bisk](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1711.02173); [LAUG](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.15262v3)). Proposed ship gate, to be confirmed by Paulo: on the human final set, over-extraction at or below 5%, per-field recall at or above 85%, marker false-flag rate at or below 5%, and no default-fill events.

## Conclusion

The 100%-to-15% gap is not a noise problem yet: the hand-written test is almost perfectly spelled, and the reader still failed, mainly by not mapping everyday objects, places and self-descriptions to fields, and by filling fields nobody stated. Real askers make that harder, because the CCDR record shows they leave JOUE, territory and dates out entirely, wrap values in "ronda os" and ranges, and argue about the law inside the question. A no-model generator can teach this, because every hard case (an inserted contradiction, a bare "concurso", an amount "com IVA", a law year that is not a start date) is constructed by code and therefore labelled with certainty. That only works if the generator samples what is asked separately from how it is phrased.

The second implication concerns the boundary D50 already draws. The 1 October 2026 reform turns many user sentences into true-or-false depending on a date, and since that date is itself something users get wrong (launch vs decisão de contratar), the extractor's honesty rests on two choices only Paulo can make: whether launch dates count as start dates, and whether the engine will read ambiguity markers. Once those are settled, the decision metric should be the over-extraction rate on human-written, human-typed and human-dictated questions, because for a deterministic law engine an invented fact does more damage than a missing one.
